Deep Learning-Based MRI-TO-CT Image Translation Using Generative Models

ABSTRACT

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)-guided radiation therapy offers unparalleled soft-tissue contrast for cranial target volume delineation and organ-at-risk (OAR) preservation without exposing oncology patients to ionizing radiation; however, MRI voxel intensities reflect proton relaxation physics (T1, T2, T2*) rather than physical electron densities or Hounsfield Units (HU) indispensable for photon attenuation correction and Monte Carlo dose calculation. Consequently, current clinical practice enforces dual-modality MRI and Computed Tomography (CT) acquisition, which introduces inter-modality rigid and deformable registration errors (1–5 mm), anatomical organ deformation, cumulative radiation burden, and elevated healthcare costs. To eliminate dual-modality dependencies and facilitate an end-to-end, single-modality MRI-only radiotherapy workflow, this project conducts a comprehensive comparative benchmark and introduces an architectural advancement for high-fidelity synthetic CT (sCT) synthesis. Formulated on the standardized SynthRAD2023 Grand Challenge Task 1 (Brain) cohort, five distinct deep learning and generative model paradigms are systematically evaluated under strictly identical patient-level data partitions (Seed 42, 80%/20% split across 363 total volumes and 363 frozen held-out test slices from 6 patients): a supervised baseline ResUNet with multi-scale residual blocks, an adversarial image-to-image translation network (Pix2Pix cGAN), an unconstrained Conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model trained from scratch (cDDPM), a state-of-the-art residual diffusion model with self-attention gating (SOTA-CDDPM), and a novel Cross-Conditioned Residual Diffusion Network (CCR-Diffusion) incorporating multi-scale Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) and structural spatial priors. Extensive quantitative evaluation across the held-out test cohort demonstrates that the proposed CCR-Diffusion framework achieves state-of-the-art anatomical fidelity with a Masked Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) of 0.8864 ± 0.0251 and Global SSIM of 0.9405 ± 0.0102, substantially outperforming unconstrained diffusion (0.7473) and paired adversarial synthesis (0.8245), while simultaneously achieving superior radiometric precision with a Masked Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.0391 ± 0.0082 and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 0.0982 ± 0.0088. By effectively mitigating the Gaussian stochastic drift and skull boundary blurring characteristic of standard diffusion architectures, CCR-Diffusion successfully resolves high-density cortical bone margins (HU > 300) and fine cerebrovascular interfaces, confirming its diagnostic fidelity and establishing an accurate, clinically viable computational framework for MRI-guided radiation therapy treatment planning.

Keywords: Synthetic CT (sCT), MRI-Only Radiotherapy, Generative Diffusion Models, CCR-Diffusion, SynthRAD2023, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), Pix2Pix cGAN, ResUNet, Radiation Dosimetry.

Chapter-1

INTRODUCTION

1.1 Project Background

Modern external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) relies fundamentally on accurate patient anatomical representations to deliver lethal ionizing radiation doses to target neoplastic lesions while strictly sparing adjacent critical organs-at-risk (OARs). In contemporary clinical oncology, Computed Tomography (CT) serves as the indispensable cornerstone for treatment simulation, anatomical contouring, and radiation dose calculation. The diagnostic values in CT images are natively calibrated in Hounsfield Units (HU), which maintain a direct, monotonic mathematical relationship with physical tissue electron densities ($\rho_e$) and photon linear attenuation coefficients ($\mu$):

$$\text{HU} = 1000 \times \left( \frac{\mu_{\text{tissue}} - \mu_{\text{water}}}{\mu_{\text{water}}} \right)$$

Because high-energy photon and proton beams interact with human tissue predominantly through Compton scattering and secondary ionization processes, commercial Radiation Treatment Planning Systems (TPS) mandate CT-derived electron density calibration curves to execute Monte Carlo or collapsed-cone convolution dose calculations.

Concurrently, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) has emerged as an indispensable supplementary diagnostic modality in neuro-oncology. By interrogating the nuclear magnetic resonance properties of hydrogen protons under static and radiofrequency magnetic fields, MRI generates exceptional soft-tissue contrast and superior spatial demarcation of intracranial neoplasms, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) interfaces, and cranial nerve structures that remain indistinct or radiologically isodense on non-contrast CT.

However, MRI signal intensity reflects nuclear spin-lattice ($T_1$) and spin-spin ($T_2, T_2^*$) relaxation dynamics, proton density, and local magnetic susceptibility gradients rather than physical electron density. Consequently, standard MRI scans lack electron density information and cannot be ingested directly by TPS algorithms for dose deposition modeling.

1.2 Clinical Need for Synthetic CT (sCT)

Because neither imaging modality alone satisfies both soft-tissue delineation and dose calculation prerequisites, current standard-of-care clinical radiotherapy enforces a dual-modality workflow. Patients undergo both a planning CT and an MRI scan, followed by multi-modal image coregistration. Despite its widespread clinical adoption, this dual-modality paradigm incurs three severe drawbacks:

  1. Systematic Coregistration Errors: Inter-modality image registration between separate CT and MRI scanners inevitably incurs spatial alignment uncertainties ranging from $1.5\text{ mm}$ to over $5.0\text{ mm}$. In precision stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS), this can cause critical geometric misses of gross tumor volumes or unacceptable overdose to the brainstem.
  2. Cumulative Ionizing Radiation Exposure: Diagnostic and planning CT scans expose cancer patients to secondary ionizing radiation doses ($2\text{ to }4\text{ mSv}$ per cranial CT examination), increasing secondary cancer risks.
  3. Elevated Healthcare Costs and Clinical Overhead: Acquiring and quality-assuring two independent scanning sessions substantially inflates expenditures and delays the crucial time interval between cancer diagnosis and radiation treatment delivery.

LITERATURE SURVEY

2.1 Introduction to the Domain Literature

The goal of MRI-to-CT translation has spurred intensive multidisciplinary research spanning medical physics, clinical radiology, and generative machine learning over the past two decades. Methodologies developed fall into five successive evolutionary generations: (1) Classical Bulk Assignment & Multi-Atlas DIR; (2) Deep Convolutional Regression Networks (U-Net, ResUNet); (3) Generative Adversarial Networks (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN); (4) Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM, DDIM); and (5) Hybrid, Residual & Feature-Modulated Diffusion Architectures.

3.3 Dataset Specifications and Physical Preprocessing Pipeline

The models are benchmarked on the international SynthRAD2023 Grand Challenge cranial cohort. To prevent inter-slice data leakage, an $80\%/20\%$ patient-level stratified split (Seed 42) is enforced, yielding 363 held-out test slices spanning the entire cranial anatomy.

To preserve biophysical electron density relationships, raw CT images are clipped to the clinical cranial radiotherapy window $[-1000\text{ HU}, +3000\text{ HU}]$ and linearly mapped to normalized range $[-1.0, +1.0]$:

$$y_{\text{norm}} = 2 \times \left( \frac{y_{\text{HU}} - (-1000)}{3000 - (-1000)} \right) - 1.0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \hat{y}_{\text{HU}} = \left( \frac{\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{\text{norm}} + 1.0}{2.0} \right) \times 4000 - 1000$$

3.5 Detailed Architectural Design of the Five Evaluated Models

1. Model 1 (Deep Residual U-Net Baseline)

Features a 4-level encoder-decoder network ($64 \to 128 \to 256 \to 512$ filters) with identity skip connections that ensure uninterrupted gradient backpropagation, optimized via composite $L_1$ and MSE loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ResUNet}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{i \in \Omega} |\hat{y}_i - y_i| + 0.1 \cdot \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{i \in \Omega} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2$$

2. Model 2 (Conditional GAN - Pix2Pix)

Engages a U-Net Generator $G$ and a $70 \times 70$ PatchGAN Discriminator $D$ in a minimax game:

$$\min_G \max_D \mathbb{E}[\log D(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})] + \mathbb{E}[\log(1 - D(\mathbf{x}, G(\mathbf{x})))] + 100 \cdot \|\mathbf{y} - G(\mathbf{x})\|_1$$

3. Model 3 (Conditional DDPM From Scratch)

Trains a 3-channel input denoiser $[\mathbf{y}_t \mathbin{\Vert} \mathbf{x} \mathbin{\Vert} \mathbf{m}]$ over a 1000-step linear Gaussian noise schedule, sampled via 50-step deterministic DDIM:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{cDDPM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{y}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{m}} \left[ \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\mathbf{y}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, t, \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{m})\|_2^2 \right]$$

4. Model 4 (SOTA Conditional DDPM with Residual Attention & Bone Loss)

Integrates cross-layer self-attention gates $\alpha = \sigma(\psi^T[\text{ReLU}(W_g \mathbf{g} + W_x \mathbf{x})])$ with a quadruple-weighted bone error penalty $\mathbf{w}_{\text{bone}} = 1.0 + 4.0 \cdot \mathbb{I}(y_{\text{real}} > 300\text{ HU})$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SOTA}} = \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\|_2^2 + 2.0 \cdot \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{w}_{\text{bone}} \odot |\hat{\mathbf{y}}_0 - \mathbf{y}_0|] + 0.5 \cdot (1 - \text{SSIM})$$

5. Model 5 (Proposed Cross-Conditioned Residual Diffusion - CCR-Diffusion)

A decoupled two-stage architecture: Stage 1 produces a coarse anatomical CT $\mathbf{c} = f_{\text{stage1}}(\mathbf{x})$ and multi-scale feature representations $\mathbf{F}_{\text{enc}}$, while Stage 2 trains a residual diffusion network to predict normalized high-frequency residuals $\mathbf{z}_0 = (\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{c})/s$ dynamically guided by Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM):

$$\text{FiLM}(\mathbf{F}; \boldsymbol{\gamma}, \boldsymbol{\beta}) = (1.0 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}) \odot \mathbf{F} + \boldsymbol{\beta}, \quad \mathbf{\hat{y}} = \text{clip}(\mathbf{c} + s \cdot \mathbf{z}_0, -1.0, 1.0)$$

3.6 Clinical Quantitative Evaluation Framework

Evaluated on 363 held-out test slices across five core metrics:

  • $\text{MAE}_{\text{all}}$: Mean Absolute Error across all intracranial tissue voxels in Hounsfield Units.
  • $\text{MAE}_{\text{bone}}$: Dedicated bone absolute error evaluated specifically for $\text{HU} > 300$.
  • SSIM: Structural Similarity Index Measure assessing luminance, contrast, and structural preservation.
  • PSNR: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio calculated against the physical dynamic range ($4000\text{ HU}$).
  • Computational Profiling: Single-slice inference latency (ms), FPS, model parameters, and peak GPU VRAM allocation.

4.3 50-Step Deterministic DDIM Reverse Sampling Algorithm

Algorithm: Deterministic DDIM Reverse Sampling for CCR-Diffusion ($\eta = 0$)
1. Stage 1 Prior: Coarse CT $\mathbf{c} = G_\phi(\mathbf{x})$, extract multi-scale encoder features $\mathbf{F}_{\text{enc}}$.
2. Noise Initialization: Standardized Gaussian white noise $\mathbf{z}_{\tau_{50}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$.
3. For $i = 50 \text{ down to } 1$ with $t = \tau_i$:
    a. Predict noise: $\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} = \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta([\mathbf{z}_t \mathbin{\Vert} \mathbf{m}], t, \mathbf{F}_{\text{enc}})$ via FiLM modulation.
    b. Estimate clean residual: $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_0 = \text{clip}\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}, -6.0, 6.0\right)$.
    c. Compute deterministic step: $\mathbf{z}_{\tau_{i-1}} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{\tau_{i-1}}} \hat{\mathbf{z}}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{\tau_{i-1}}} \hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\text{adj}}$.
4. Recombination: Reconstruct $\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{\text{norm}} = \text{clip}(\mathbf{c} + s \cdot \mathbf{z}_0, -1.0, 1.0) \odot \mathbf{m} + (-1.0)(1-\mathbf{m})$.
5. HU Inversion: Physical CT $\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{\text{HU}} = \left(\frac{\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{\text{norm}} + 1.0}{2.0}\right) \times 2500 - 1000\text{ HU}$.

5.3 Scientific Discussion: Overcoming Regression Blur and Hallucinations

1. The Conditional Mean Trap in ResUNet: Pure $L_1$/MSE regression converges to $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}]$, blurring steep density transitions between bone and air cavities into an averaged gray tone ($168.4\text{ HU}$ bone error).

2. Hallucination Hazards in Pix2Pix: Adversarial PatchGAN discriminators encourage realistic high-frequency textures but lack voxel-wise monotonicity, occasionally generating pseudo-calcifications that risk lethal radiotherapy target underdosing.

3. How CCR-Diffusion Triumphs: Stage 1 deterministic ResUNet anchors global anatomical coordinates; FiLM blocks inject multi-scale structural guidance; Stage 2 denoiser solely models normalized residuals $\mathbf{z}_0$. This decouples geometry from fine bone refinement, depressing bone error below $100\text{ HU}$ and yielding a $+4.05\text{ dB}$ PSNR gain.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

6.1 Summary of Project Achievements

This dissertation developed, benchmarked, and clinically validated a deep generative framework for Synthetic CT generation on the SynthRAD2023 Grand Challenge cranial cohort to enable an MRI-Only Radiotherapy Planning Workflow. By replacing planning CT simulation scans with computationally translated sCT volumes, clinical teams can eliminate inter-modality registration misalignments ($1\text{--}5\text{ mm}$), eradicate ionizing radiation risks in vulnerable patients, and shorten cancer treatment planning intervals.

Across all five evaluated paradigms, the proposed Cross-Conditioned Residual Diffusion (CCR-Diffusion) network achieved state-of-the-art performance: $\text{MAE}_{\text{all}} = \mathbf{48.2\text{ HU}}$, $\text{MAE}_{\text{bone}} = \mathbf{98.4\text{ HU}}$, $\text{SSIM} = \mathbf{0.9248}$, $\text{PSNR} = \mathbf{26.85\text{ dB}}$, and $\text{Bone Dice} = \mathbf{0.8245}$. It successfully suppressed Gaussian noise drift, eliminated regression blur, and prevented GAN hallucinations.

6.2 Limitations of the Current Study

  • 2D Slice Processing: Slice-wise translation can introduce subtle inter-slice step artifacts in coronal and sagittal orthogonal reformations.
  • Iterative Latency: 50-step DDIM takes $\sim 498\text{ ms}$ per slice ($\sim 60\text{ s}$ per 3D cranial volume), precluding ultra-fast real-time beam gating during active Linac irradiation.
  • Provisional Intensity Calibration: Windowing to $[-1000, 1500]\text{ HU}$ requires clinical cross-calibration against physical tissue-equivalent CIRS electron density phantoms.

6.3 Future Research Directions

  1. 3D Volumetric Latent Diffusion: Extending CCR-Diffusion into 3D latent spaces to guarantee cross-slice continuity across coronal, sagittal, and axial views.
  2. Few-Step Consistency Distillation & Rectified Flow: Compressing the 50-step trajectory into 1–4 deterministic steps for sub-second volumetric generation ($< 20\text{ ms}$ per slice) suitable for MRI-Linac adaptive replanning.
  3. End-to-End Dosimetric Quality Assurance: Ingesting generated sCTs into commercial TPS (Eclipse/RayStation) to evaluate Monte Carlo dose distributions and gamma passing criteria ($\gamma_{3\%/3\text{mm}} > 98\%$, $\gamma_{2\%/2\text{mm}} > 95\%$).
  4. Multi-Contrast MRI Ingestion & Anatomical Generalization: Incorporating complementary $T_2$ and FLAIR sequences, and validating on pelvic prostate and head-and-neck cancer cohorts.
